## Summary in six lines

1. **The strongest baseline we have found is another language model's activations on the same text.**
   OLMo-3-7B, which shares no lineage with our model, ties us on all five targets. Text baselines and
   sentence embedders are well behind. This is the headline for the meeting, not TF-IDF.
2. **A frontier model in context ties us on the distribution targets and loses on length.** GPT-6 Luna,
   full 1,178-row run, 8 configurations, $17.86, zero parse failures.
3. **More examples make in-context prediction worse**, in two unrelated model families. Zero-shot is
   the best in-context setting every time.
4. **Shortening the examples helps a little and changes nothing.** It buys 4x more examples (k=93 to
   k=400) and k=400 is the worst setting of all.
5. **Hiding how far along the chain is costs nothing on entropy, P(mode) and commitment, but hurts both
   length targets significantly.** That is the result you would want: the model uses the depth label
   exactly where the label is informative.
6. **The logistic C-grid loose end is closed and the published 0.741 stands** — but closing it surfaced
   two things worth reporting: commitment accuracy at the 90% cut is just the base rate, and under AUC
   our edge over TF-IDF on that target nearly vanishes.

## Ask 1 — in context with a smaller prompt

New flag `--ex-sents N` in `src/baselines/icl_predict.py` shortens each **example's** reasoning to its
last N sentences and keeps the question and choices in full. The query is untouched. Gemma-4-31B,
thinking off, N=2, k ∈ {10, 30, 93, 400}. Job 4942955, 1 h 11 min on 2 GPUs, 20/20 files, 0 parse
failures. Raw output `runs/goal2/gpqa25/icl/raw/gemma4_31b_off_s2_k<k>_fold<f>.csv`.

Shortening gives examples at **8.5%** of full length, and lifts the longest prompt that fits from
250,604 tokens at k=93 to 143,575 tokens at k=400.

Mean over the 10 to 90% cut points, shortened vs full at the same k:

| k | entropy, short | entropy, full | length, short | length, full |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 10 | 0.193 | 0.176 | −0.060 | −0.162 |
| 30 | 0.194 | 0.144 | +0.014 | −0.006 |
| 93 | 0.171 | 0.158 | +0.039 | +0.041 |
| 400 | 0.156 | not reachable | — | — |

**Shortened scores higher in 5 of 6 same-k cells.** Only the k=10 length cell is significant on its
own (+0.102), and 5 of 6 same-sign is not significant by a sign test, so treat the improvement as
suggestive rather than established. Every shortened configuration stays significantly below our probe
(entropy −0.115 to −0.153) and below Gemma at k=0 (0.262).

**Answer to the ask:** smaller prompts buy 4x more examples and a small, mostly-not-significant
improvement at fixed k. Zero-shot remains the best in-context setting by a wide margin.

An earlier internal note said shortening *destroys* the length prediction. That was backwards and has
been corrected. What collapses the length prediction is having **any** examples at all: 0.283 at k=0
falls to about 0 at k≥10.

## Ask 2 — frontier model

GPT-6 Luna (`gpt-6-luna`, reasoning high), full 1,178-row subsample, all 8 configurations. Run from the
login node with `src/baselines/icl_openai.py run --model gpt-6-luna --full`. **$17.86, 9,424 calls, 0
parse failures**, cache 91.8k of 92k on k=50. Log `runs/logs/round5/icl_luna_full{,2,3}.log`.

Mean over 10 to 90%, calibrated, against ridge on the same rows (0.309 / 0.261 / 0.563 / 0.328 / 0.727):

| configuration | entropy | P(mode) | rem. length | length spread | committed |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| k=0 (best) | 0.294 | 0.233 | 0.455 | 0.122 | 0.718 |
| k=50 | 0.255 | 0.219 | 0.272 | 0.098 | 0.720 |
| k=10 | 0.216 | 0.187 | 0.192 | 0.067 | 0.721 |

Every configuration ties ridge on entropy, P(mode) and commitment, and is significantly worse on both

Two run bugs were found and fixed, both recorded in `learnings.md`:

  retry, so the filter is non-deterministic.
- The cache-miss guard used a single counter with a threshold of 2 for the whole run, so 2 transient
  misses in 3,833 calls stopped it. The counter now resets per fold.

## Ask 3 — remove the checkpoint location

This needed a new prompt version, because the one from round 3 did not do what the ask said.

| version | examples show `[checkpoint X%]` | **query** shows it |
|---|---|---|
| `ckpt` | yes | yes |
| `nockpt` (round 3) | no | **yes** |
| `blind` (new) | no | **no** |

`nockpt` hid the label on the examples only, so the model still knew how far along the prefix it was
being asked about. `blind` removes it everywhere, including from the instruction text. Defined at
`src/baselines/icl_openai.py:31-34`, built at `:111-123`.

Result, from `runs/goal2/gpqa25/sweep/_icl/ci_scores.csv`:

| target | effect of hiding the depth |
|---|---|
| entropy, P(mode), commitment | no change at any k |
| remaining length | R² falls by 0.119 / 0.239 / 0.166 at k = 0 / 10 / 50, significant at every k |
| length spread | R² falls by 0.059 / 0.095 / 0.071 at k = 0 / 10 / 50, significant at every k |

**This is a good result rather than a worrying one.** The depth label is genuinely informative for how
much reasoning is left and genuinely uninformative for how uncertain the answer distribution is. Hiding
it hurts exactly the first and leaves the second alone, which is a working sanity check on the whole
in-context setup.

A side observation worth having ready: `nockpt` (hide on examples, keep on query) scores **higher** than
showing it everywhere, at both k=10 and k=50 on all five targets. The depth labels on the examples were
mild clutter; the one on the query was doing all the work.

Note that the earlier 100-row check reported "hiding changes nothing". That was right only for the
non-length targets, and the full run is what should be quoted.

`runs/goal2/gpqa25/sweep/_master/` scores **22 approaches on identical rows, folds and error bars**
(`src/viz/master_table.py`). 4,911 to 4,915 rows common to all 22 per cell. It reproduces every
published number exactly (ours 0.289 / 0.251 / 0.524 / 0.298 / 0.741; OLMo 0.282; TF-IDF 0.237; L20-22
0.293).

The inventory:

- Our probe, plus the two text baselines: word counts and prefix length
- **4 other models:** Qwen2.5-Instruct, Qwen2.5-Math, OLMo-3-7B, Mistral-7B
- **5 sentence embedders:** Qwen3-Embedding-8B, F2LLM-v2-8B, UltraBERT, BGE-M3, Granite
- **7 probe types:** two MLP sizes, two random forests, gradient boosting, two PCA variants
- **3 layer choices:** layer 14, layers 13 to 15, layers 20 to 22

Only Qwen2.5-Instruct and Qwen2.5-Math are the same family as ours. OLMo-3 and Mistral are unrelated.

**Findings.** Only **layers 20-22 concatenated** beats us, by +0.005 entropy and +0.004 P(mode), both
significant and both tiny. Three approaches tie us on all five targets: Qwen2.5-Instruct,
Qwen2.5-Math and **OLMo-3-7B**. MLP h=256 ties on 4 of 5. Everything else is significantly below us.

**So the answer to "what is a strong baseline" is: another language model's activations on the same
text.** Not the text baselines, which is where earlier write-ups put the emphasis. OLMo-3 tying us
while sharing no lineage with our model is the uncomfortable part, and it connects directly to the
question-difficulty finding from round 4.

A separate table, `runs/goal2/gpqa25/sweep/_icl/` (`src/viz/icl_table.py`), scores the in-context runs
by the same method on the 1,178-row ICL subsample. It is deliberately separate: folding the ICL runs
into `_master` would shrink all 22 approaches down to those rows and widen every interval. It was
validated against the published R1 numbers and matches exactly.

## Ask 7 — is LoRA working

Yes. This was settled in R8 (round 2) and the numbers were re-verified this round.

The first answer looked bad because LoRA's own prediction head was below ridge. Training the same head
on the **frozen** stored states with the same recipe reproduced the deficit, which showed the head's
training recipe was the problem, not LoRA. Ridge on the LoRA-adapted layer-21 states does beat ridge on
the stored states:

| cut | entropy: LoRA / stored / diff | rem. length: LoRA / stored / diff |
|---|---|---|
| 10% | 0.281 / 0.278 / +0.003 n.s. | 0.635 / 0.626 / +0.009 |
| 30% | 0.307 / 0.295 / +0.012 | 0.603 / 0.587 / +0.016 |
| 50% | 0.309 / 0.298 / +0.012 | 0.574 / 0.548 / +0.026 |
| 70% | 0.312 / 0.302 / +0.009 | 0.503 / 0.484 / +0.019 |
| 90% | 0.291 / 0.270 / +0.021 | 0.399 / 0.375 / +0.024 |

Every difference is significant except entropy at 10%. 12 GPU-hours in total. Re-verified 2026-10-27:
the ten differences independently from `runs/goal2/gpqa25/lora/ci_f0.*.csv`, and the LoRA-side
absolutes by recomputing `r2_score` on `runs/goal2/gpqa25/lora/f0*_L21_r16_ridge/preds.csv`.

## Loose end closed — the logistic C grid

The published grid `logspace(-4, 1, 6)` picked C = 1e-4, its bottom edge, in **18 of 25 folds**, so every
`committed_by_here` number we had ever quoted came from edge-pinned fits. Extended to 1e-8 (10 points),
probe renamed `logistic_deep`, rerun on all three reference features. Job 4941954, 36 min.
`runs/goal2/gpqa25/sweep/ref_logistic_deep/`.

**The published 0.741 stands:** ours 0.7409 to 0.7403, a change of −0.0006. TF-IDF and prefix length are
identical to 4 decimal places. The edge-pinning was a **flat region**, not a truncated optimum: the extra
folds that picked 1e-8 scored very slightly worse, exactly as a flat but noisy inner-CV surface predicts.
The probe is not degenerate (two classes predicted, AUC 0.78 to 0.80 at 10 to 70%).

Two things this surfaced that **should be reported**:

1. **At the 90% cut, commitment accuracy is just the base rate.** Majority class 0.7609, ours 0.7583,
   TF-IDF 0.7595, prefix length 0.7609. The mean majority baseline over 10 to 90% is 0.6275, so the
   0.741 headline is a real +0.11 lift, but it is carried by 10 to 70% and the 90% cell contributes
   nothing.
2. **Under AUC our edge over TF-IDF on this target nearly vanishes:** 0.7612 vs 0.7581, a gap of +0.003,
   and TF-IDF is **ahead** at 90% (0.701 vs 0.656). Report AUC alongside accuracy for commitment from
   here on.

## Conventions this round reinforced

- **Score within each cut point, then average. Never pool across depth.** Pooled as the literature
  pools, prefix length gets 0.718 against our 0.716.
- **Report both answer entropy and remaining length.** They behave differently often enough that one
  number alone misleads: in-context models tie us on entropy and lose badly on length; the degree-2
  kernel gains on length and nothing on entropy.
- **For commitment, report AUC alongside accuracy**, and give the majority-class baseline.
- Units: R² for entropy, P(mode), remaining length and length spread; **accuracy** for commitment. The
  two sit side by side in every table, which is a real readability trap.
