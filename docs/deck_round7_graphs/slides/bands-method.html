Why MSE rather than R squared inside a band

  • R squared inside a band is scored against that band's own variance. Where the truth is nearly constant, the denominator collapses and the number goes sharply negative even when the predictions are close.
  • That is exactly what produced the two embarrassing cells: GPQA very easy at -1.090 and MMLU-Pro easy at -1.049.
  • Mean squared error has the same units in every band, so the bands can be read against each other.
  • Each graph carries two reference lines: guessing that band's own average, and guessing the whole set's average. Below the first line means the probe is adding something inside the band.
Both the as-stored and the offset-recentred R squared columns are kept in the underlying CSVs, so nothing was thrown away in the switch. The bottom row of each figure is the offset-free view, a plain within-band correlation, which is the most forgiving reading and is included so the MSE panel is not the only evidence.
