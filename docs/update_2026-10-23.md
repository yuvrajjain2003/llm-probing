# Round 4 update — dataset redundancy, the MLP gap, and where we stand in the literature

_2026-10-23. Three parallel investigations run overnight 2026-10-22/23. Every number here was
read from a file on disk; paths are given so any line can be re-checked. Two items are marked
PENDING and are the only things not yet settled._

---

## Summary in five lines

1. **The 25 chains per question are not redundant — but they were the wrong thing to buy.** The same
   probe quality is reachable with **2.5× fewer rollouts** by spreading the budget over more questions.
2. **The probe is overwhelmingly a question-difficulty reader.** It captures 0.3–12% of the
   within-question signal that is demonstrably there, and this is not a noise limit or a
   training-objective artefact.
3. **Linear probes are not quite optimal after all.** A degree-2 kernel beats ridge by +0.0081 and an
   ensembled MLP by +0.0039 (both significant, and both survive comparison against an oracle-tuned
   ridge) — but only on remaining reasoning length. Entropy, p_mode and commitment are linear-ceilinged.
4. **The long-standing "MLPs never beat ridge" result was a training-recipe artefact.** The recipe
   behind every prior MLP and LoRA-head number lands at cosine 0.865 from the ridge optimum.
5. **Our published numbers are competitive** once the log-scale and depth-pooling confounds in the
   literature are accounted for — and three of our methodological choices are things nobody else does.

## Idea 1 — how redundant is the dataset, and does it taint the probes?

Spec `docs/plan_redundancy.md`; outputs `runs/goal2/gpqa25/sweep/_redundancy/`.

**Trust check first.** The decomposition reproduces `sweep/ref_fixed/results.csv` in **75/75 cells to
≤1e-6**, so these numbers sit on the same rows, folds and predictions as our published results.

### Cosine similarity (the measurement requested directly)

Separation AUC = P(a same-question pair is more similar than a random pair). 0.5 means the 25 chains
are no more alike than two unrelated chains; 1.0 means perfect clustering by question.

| preprocessing | 10% | 30% | 50% | 70% | 90% |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| raw | 0.696 | 0.660 | 0.649 | 0.643 | 0.639 |
| centered | 0.780 | 0.730 | 0.712 | 0.700 | 0.692 |
| standardised (what the probe sees) | 0.802 | 0.752 | 0.732 | 0.719 | 0.711 |

Moderate clustering, not near-duplication. **Centering is essential**: raw cosine is ~0.67 within
question against ~0.63 across, so raw figures look uniformly high and say nothing. Two further results:

- **The text is far more question-clustered than the activations.** TF-IDF cosine separates
  within- from across-question at **AUC 1.000** at 30–90%. The hidden states are substantially
  *less* redundant than the prefixes they encode.
- **Chain identity is nearly invisible.** Same chain at two depths versus a *different* chain of the
  same question gives AUC 0.50–0.54. Once you know the question, which chain it is barely registers.

### The labels are heavily question-clustered

ICC — the share of variance between questions, i.e. the ceiling for any question-level predictor:

| target | 10% | 50% | 90% |
|---|---|---|---|
| entropy | 0.811 | 0.590 | 0.405 |
| rem_len_mean | 0.925 | 0.735 | 0.409 |

### The probe reads almost only that

| checkpoint | pooled R² | between-question | within-question R² | within Pearson |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 10% | 0.278 | 0.398 | −0.265 | 0.042 |
| 50% | 0.298 | 0.529 | −0.056 | 0.166 |
| 90% | 0.270 | 0.545 | +0.064 | 0.279 |

Two candidate excuses were tested and both fail:

- **Not a noise limit.** 25-rollout estimation error accounts for only 6–45% of within-question label
  variance. Real within-question signal exists, with noise-corrected ceilings of 0.54–0.94. The probe
  reaches **0.3–12%** of it. (Cross-check: the overall 25-mean ceiling computed here, 0.985→0.966 for
  `rem_len_mean`, matches an independent derivation in the literature review exactly.)
- **Not a training-objective artefact.** Training ridge directly on question-demeaned targets does
  *not* recover the signal — it is significantly *worse* on entropy and p_mode at 30–90%, and the
  chosen α runs to the grid's upper edge of 1e6, i.e. the fit shrinks to nothing rather than finding
  something finer. TF-IDF is equal or better than activations within-question.

**Conclusion: the probe separates easy questions from hard ones, with a small chain-specific component
concentrated at the deepest checkpoints.**

### The allocation finding — the most actionable result of the round

Mean R² over all five checkpoints, same row budget split different ways:

| rows | questions | CoTs each | entropy | rem_len_mean |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 300 | 12 | 25 | 0.063 | 0.266 |
| 300 | 30 | 10 | 0.168 | 0.355 |
| 300 | 60 | 5 | 0.182 | 0.386 |
| **300** | **150** | **2** | **0.202** | **0.407** |
| 744 | 30 | 25 | 0.196 | 0.406 |
| 750 | 75 | 10 | 0.227 | 0.451 |
| **750** | **150** | **5** | **0.243** | **0.463** |

> **150 questions × 2 chains (300 rows) matches 30 questions × 25 chains (744 rows)** — entropy
> 0.202 vs 0.196, remaining length 0.407 vs 0.406. Same quality, **2.5× fewer rollouts.**

The chains are *not* worthless: holding questions at 198, going from 1 chain to 25 roughly doubles
entropy R² (0.152 → 0.298 at the 50% checkpoint), monotonically and with no plateau. But per unit of
rollout compute — the dominant cost of this project — questions buy far more. Within the tested range
the question axis shows no sign of saturating while the chain axis clearly does.

**Recommendation for the next collection: many more questions, 2–5 chains each.**

## Idea 2 — close the MLP/ridge gap

Spec `docs/plan_mlp_gap.md`; outputs `runs/goal2/gpqa25/sweep/_mlpgap/`.

### The cause of three weeks of "MLPs never beat ridge"

Across **80 fits** (5 checkpoints × 4 targets × 5 folds), a properly optimised linear head reproduces
ridge's closed-form solution at **cosine 1.0000 exactly**. The step-4a recipe used in every prior MLP
and LoRA-head experiment sits at **cosine 0.865 mean, 0.630 worst**, relative L2 error 0.600 mean and
2.83 worst, and costs **0.008–0.025 R²** depending on target. Its *score* gap looked trivial while its
*weight vector* was badly misaligned, which is exactly why comparing scores alone hid this.

### The ceiling, from closed-form kernels (no optimiser to argue about)

| target | pooled gain over linear | significant? |
|---|---|---|
| **`rem_len_mean`** | **+0.0081 [+0.0058, +0.0103]** | yes |
| `rem_len_std` | +0.0018 [−0.0026, +0.0058] | no — but **+0.0124 at 90% alone**, significant |
| `p_mode` | +0.0016 [−0.0005, +0.0037] | no |
| `committed_by_here` | +0.0012 [−0.0007, +0.0031] | no |
| `answer_entropy_bits_mm` | −0.0001 [−0.0017, +0.0015] | no |

`rem_len_mean` per checkpoint: +0.0046, −0.0005, **+0.0109***, **+0.0103***, **+0.0150*** at
10/30/50/70/90% — **growing with depth**. This converges with idea 1's independent finding that
within-question signal rises from ~0 at 10% to a maximum at 90%: two unrelated analyses locating the
same structure in the same place, on precisely the two targets that depend on the individual chain
rather than on the question.

### The MLP does beat ridge, narrowly — final numbers

Pooled over 10–90% on `rem_len_mean`, same rows, same folds, paired:

| method | vs project reference ridge | vs **oracle-tuned** ridge |
|---|---|---|
| MLP (`ens5_resid_h64_sq`) | **+0.0039 [+0.0018, +0.0063]** | **+0.0026 [+0.0002, +0.0050]** |
| degree-2 kernel (`krr_honest_joint`) | **+0.0081 [+0.0058, +0.0103]** | **+0.0067 [+0.0044, +0.0089]** |

All four significant. **The MLP captures about half the available nonlinear structure; closed-form
kernel ridge captures all of it.**

**The win is not an artefact of a mistuned baseline.** Ridge's mistuning was measured per cell
(best-on-test fixed α minus per-fold CV) against the MLP's gain:

| checkpoint | 10% | 30% | 50% | 70% | 90% |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| ridge mistuning | −0.0004 | **0.0000** | +0.0071 | **0.0000** | **0.0000** |
| MLP gain | +0.0021* | +0.0021* | +0.0045* | +0.0066* | +0.0043 |

Four of five checkpoints have *exactly zero* mistuning and the MLP wins significantly at three of
them. The one contaminated cell (50%) is where its margin is *smallest* relative to the mistuning —
the opposite of what a tuning artefact looks like.

**Three things were required to get there**, all absent from the prior work: fitting on
**cross-fitted** residuals rather than jointly, a **square activation** matching the degree-2
structure actually present, and **seed ensembling** — without which the effect is undetectable
(single-seed is significant at *zero* checkpoints, the 5-seed ensemble at four).

**Why ensembling is the lever.** The selector declines the MLP in 70% of fold-fits for single models
but only 44% for the 5-seed ensemble. Seed-averaging converts an on/off decision into a *partial
blend* toward ridge — which is exactly the right move in a variance-limited regime.

**And why the skip connection could never climb** (instrumented at full scale: the nonlinear branch is
in the optimiser in all 20 fits, 100% of ReLU units live, no dead paths):

| regime | ‖W1‖ init→final | ‖g(X)‖/sd(y) | train R² | test R² |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| L-BFGS, L2 selected | 4.62 → **4.9e-05** | ~0 | 0.726 | 0.548 |
| same, L2 forced to 1e2 | 4.62 → **0.894** | **0.97** | **0.9995** | **0.300** |

g *does* move, and moves to zero, because zero is the regularised optimum. Force the penalty weak and
it moves enormously and test R² collapses to 0.30. **There is no setting between "crushed to zero" and
"catastrophic overfit"** — which is the clearest single picture of the variance-limited regime we have.

For context on that regime: ridge uses only **440 of 3,584 directions** (12.3%, range 206–860) at
n_train = 3,931.

Why it ties ridge everywhere else: **selection.** The inner CV picks the pure-linear fallback in 26%
of folds and trains zero epochs in another 44% — the MLP contributes nothing in **70% of folds**. In
the skip-connection variant the nonlinear branch does move, and moves *to zero*, because zero is the
regularised optimum.

### Negative results worth keeping

| attempt | outcome |
|---|---|
| 1-SE α stabiliser | **−0.016** — forty times worse than the +0.0004 instability it was meant to fix. Do not use. |
| explicit CV-chosen L2 instead of early stopping | +0.001 vs +0.005; early stopping genuinely wins here |
| sketched quadratic features (top-128 PCA pairwise products) | +0.0002 vs the kernel's +0.0046 — the quadratic signal is **not** in the leading PCA directions |
| layers 14 and 28 on demeaned targets | both worse than layer 21 |

**α-selection stability**, measured because one cell looked anomalous: per-fold CV costs only **+0.0004
mean** versus the best fixed α (max +0.0071, at `rem_len_mean` 50%), and in 14/20 cells all five folds
agree. Our reference probe is fine. The relevant caveat is narrower: bootstrap CIs are computed with
fitted models held fixed, so they do not model α-selection noise, which makes **+0.01-scale paired
effects less certain than their intervals imply** — relevant to the low end of the LoRA gains.

## Idea 3 — what the literature actually says

Spec `docs/plan_literature.md`; outputs `runs/goal2/gpqa25/sweep/_litreview/`.

**Nothing in the paper list was fabricated.** Seven papers verified (a seventh was found during
verification).

### The comparison was rigged against us

DyCon (ICML 2026, arXiv 2606.07108v2) publishes **three scales for the same fits** in Table 8,
including on our exact model, R1-Distill-Qwen-7B: log1p+MinMax **0.642**, MinMax-only **0.522**, raw
**0.501**. So the log transform is worth **+0.14, measured**, min-max is affine and cannot change R²
at all, and the only directly comparable published number for our model is **0.501 against our 0.524**
— on harder data (GPQA-Diamond vs MATH).

**And their split leaks.** Their released code splits at the *step-event* level
(`train_remain_length_reg_auto_layer.py` L712: `train_test_split` over `np.arange(N_events)`), with no
`groups=` argument and no grouped splitter anywhere. The shipped metadata records 83,342 events from
600 single-CoT trajectories — about **139 steps per trajectory, scattered randomly across train, val
and test**, so steps sharing almost all of their prefix text sit on both sides. Their 0.501 and 0.642
are therefore optimistic relative to a problem-level split, and relative to our question-level folds.
Quote the number with this caveat rather than dropping it. The +0.14 log measurement is unaffected,
since raw and log rows share the same split.

Their **effective sample size is ~600 trajectories against 83,342 nominal rows** — a ~139× gap with no
clustering correction anywhere. That is precisely the critique we applied to ourselves in idea 1, and
we corrected for it; they did not. One provenance note: the published Table 8 pair is not exactly
reproducible from the shipped metadata (R² 0.642 matches the *validation* value, MAE 0.063 matches the
*post-refit test* value), though all candidates lie in 0.642–0.651, so the magnitude holds.

Further, the **single-trajectory sampling ceiling** is 0.731→0.538 raw across our checkpoints, versus
~0.97–0.99 for our 25-continuation mean. So DyCon's 0.642 is **near-optimal for its target**, and our
0.524 is ~53% of achievable. The honest framing is not "we predict better" but "we predict a different
target under stricter scoring."

### What nobody else does

- **Nobody predicts answer-distribution properties** — entropy over sampled answers, p_mode, or the
  spread of the continuation distribution. One paper (*Reasoning Strength Planning*) does use a
  multi-sample *length* target: a mean over 8 rollouts, **confirmed from its released code**. So the
  stronger claim that every paper predicts a single trajectory is **wrong and must not be used**. Our
  K=25 at five depths against their K=8 at the prompt only is a difference of degree, not novelty.
  **`rem_len_std` is the sharpest case and survives untouched**: their mean-of-8 discards exactly the
  spread we predict, and the quantity is not expressible for a single-trajectory label at all.
- **0 of 6 run an other-model control.** All attribute their results to reasoning-specific internal
  planning. **Our OLMo tie is therefore a contribution, not an embarrassment** — we ran the control
  the field skipped.
- **0 of 6 report a trivial-scalar baseline, and none reports a confidence interval on any prediction
  metric.** Our question bootstrap is ahead of the field.

### The warning that comes with it

Pooled across depth — the way these papers pool — `rem_len_mean` gives **act_L21 0.716 against
prefix_len 0.718** (verified independently from `ref_fixed/preds*.csv`). A single scalar matches our
3,584-dim probe. We must never pool across depth, and their headline numbers are exposed to the same
confound unexamined.

Also: *Tele-Lens* makes flatness-across-depth its **headline finding**, so our flat R² across
checkpoints is a replication rather than a defect.

## What I would put in the paper

1. Prefix representations predict properties of the future continuation distribution, and beat
   bag-of-words and prefix length — but **the signal is predominantly question difficulty**, with only
   a small chain-specific component that grows with depth. State this with the variance decomposition.
2. **The same signal is available from any competent 7B transformer** (OLMo-3 ties us on all five
   targets), and no prior work in this area ran that control.
3. **Published R² values in this area are not comparable across papers** without fixing log scaling
   and depth pooling, no paper reports a trivial-scalar baseline, and the one paper with a directly
   comparable number splits at the step level with ~139 correlated rows per trajectory on both sides.
   We can demonstrate all of it, and we are the only ones who corrected for effective sample size.
4. **Score within checkpoint, never pooled** — pooled, a single scalar matches a 3,584-dim probe.
5. Ridge is near-optimal but not optimal: **closed-form degree-2 kernel ridge is the better probe**
   (+0.0081, and +0.0067 even against an oracle-tuned ridge), with an ensembled MLP reaching about
   half of that. Both confined to remaining length — entropy, p_mode and commitment are
   linear-ceilinged and no amount of model engineering will change that. Report it that way.
6. **Design guidance**: for a fixed rollout budget, more questions beats more chains by ~2.5×.

## Corrections to our own prior claims

- "We only beat prefix length at 70–90% on remaining length" — we also win at **50%** (0.5483 vs 0.5329).
- The a-priori heavy-tail argument for log-scaling is weak for *our* target (`rem_len_mean` skew only
  0.42–1.89; `log1p` over-corrects into left skew). Cite DyCon's measured +0.14 instead.
- A suspicion that DyCon selected layers on test was **wrong** — their selection uses a validation
  split, refit on train+val, reported on test. The questionable selection is *Emergent Response
  Planning*'s, which grids over hidden sizes *and* layers and reports test scores for the best.
- "Ridge is the best probe" needs the qualification in point 5 above.
