"It is just counting tokens" is an artefact of the raw scale

On the raw scale prefix length is close to our probe everywhere, and it actually beats us at two of the five cuts on GPQA and three of five on GlobalOpinionQA. On the log scale our probe wins at every cut on all three. Ours minus prefix length goes +0.039 to +0.164 on GPQA, +0.044 to +0.254 on MMLU-Pro, and -0.013 not significant to +0.521 on GlobalOpinionQA.

Be precise if challenged: averaged over cuts, prefix length is never actually ahead on the raw scale, it is close and significantly behind (GPQA 0.485 against our 0.524, MMLU-Pro 0.662 against 0.706). Where it genuinely beats us is per cut: the 10 and 30 percent cuts on GPQA, and the 30, 50 and 70 percent cuts on GlobalOpinionQA. It never beats us at any cut on MMLU-Pro. Reason: raw R squared rewards fitting a handful of very long rows, which a token count does well. Log and winsorised targets agree, so it is the tail and not the transform. Convention going forward: quote both scales and never compare a log R squared to a raw one. This also means GlobalOpinionQA is roughly level with GPQA on length once the tail is handled, 0.628 against 0.652. Method, now that it is off the figure: same rows, same folds, same five cut points, refitted on each scale. Bars are 95% question-bootstrap intervals, and on MMLU-Pro they are smaller than the markers, so they are invisible rather than absent. Exactly one marker in the figure is hollow: prefix length on raw remaining length on GlobalOpinionQA, where it nominally beats our probe by 0.013 with an interval from -0.21 to +0.15. That single cell is the whole of the 'prefix length is as good as the probe' objection.
