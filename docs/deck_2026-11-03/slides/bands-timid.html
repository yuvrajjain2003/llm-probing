Neither of us is predicting a flat number, but both are far too timid

A predictor that had learned nothing inside a band would show zero spread. Both of us sit at roughly half the real spread in every band on all three datasets. The real failure is at the easy end, where the truth drops sharply and neither of us follows it down.

This answers the obvious objection, that a low R squared inside a band might just mean both methods are emitting the band average. They are not: predicted spread is about half the truth's, not zero. On GPQA the predicted standard deviation is 0.26 to 0.30 bits against a true 0.54 to 0.57, on MMLU-Pro 0.31 to 0.39 against 0.59 to 0.73, on GlobalOpinionQA 0.21 to 0.25 against 0.42 to 0.57. The means tell the sharper story: on the very easy questions GPQA's truth is 0.10 bits while both of us say about 0.40, and MMLU-Pro's is 0.14 while both say about 0.29. So the error is not timidity alone, it is systematic over-prediction of uncertainty on questions the model finds easy. Shrinkage of this kind is what ridge regularisation produces by design, and the frontier model, which is calibrated per fold, shows the same shape, which suggests it is a property of the task rather than of our estimator. Method: questions are split into five difficulty bands and every approach is rescored inside each band, on identical rows, within each cut point and then averaged. Bars are 95% question bootstrap intervals, 2,000 draws. The top row is OFFSET CORRECTED: the stored predictions come from a fit on all questions and were never recentred per band, so an uncorrected band score is charged for an offset the full-set fit never carried. Recentring costs one degree of freedom and leaves the slope alone, so under-confidence is still penalised. The bottom row is the within-cut correlation, which has no offset at all and is the most forgiving view. Intervals inside a band are wide by construction, roughly 100 questions each, so it is the agreement across all five bands that carries the result, not any single band. Rows are the in-context row set, one chain per question, so every approach including the frontier model is scored on exactly the same rows.
