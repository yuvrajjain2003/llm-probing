  • TF-IDF does not in fact tie us. On all 2,555 questions we are ahead by 0.036, significant. The tie on the previous slide is the 500-question subset: the same power problem as the frontier model.
  • What does tie us is the question text with no prefix, and only on raw entropy. Two thirds of that is option counting: options run 2 to 18 here and entropy cannot exceed log2(options). Normalise it away and we lead the text by 0.041.
  • The questions are near-duplicates of each other. Median similarity to the nearest question in another fold is 0.76, against 0.24 on MMLU-Pro and 0.13 on GPQA. Same survey item, many countries.

Every number here was recomputed this week, row level, same folds, 2,000-draw question bootstrap. Full set, ours minus TF-IDF on raw entropy +0.0356 [+0.0242,+0.0469] significant, against -0.0148 [-0.0512,+0.0219] not significant on the 500-question subset. Question text plus choices is +0.0045 [-0.0129,+0.0217] versus us on raw entropy, a real tie, but -0.0405 [-0.0561,-0.0262] on normalised entropy, significantly behind. On MMLU-Pro question text gets 0.136 against our 0.410 and on GPQA 0.116 against 0.289, both significant, so this is specific to this dataset. Option count: a quadratic in log2(n_options) alone scores 0.143 out-of-fold here versus 0.004 on MMLU-Pro, where almost every question has ten options. And the model leaks the option count into its own reasoning, so TF-IDF can read it: ridge on prefix TF-IDF predicts log2(n_options) at R squared 0.686. One honest point to volunteer: option counting explains the LEVEL but not the closeness, because normalising lowers us and TF-IDF by almost the same amount and leaves our lead unchanged at +0.034. On duplication, measured after stripping the shared survey instruction: 60 percent of questions have a neighbour above 0.7 cosine in another fold and 13 percent above 0.9, and those near twins carry similar labels, mean entropy difference 0.66 standard deviations against 1.08 for random pairs. That is the regime a lexical model is best in.
