Takeaways, and what we would like to decide

What we now believe

  • The frontier tie was a power problem. On 2,500 questions our probe beats it, on all five targets on MMLU-Pro.
  • The probe is a question-level reader, not merely a difficulty reader. GPQA's difficulty collapse does not replicate on either new dataset.
  • Removing difficulty widens our lead over text on both large datasets, and only erases it on GPQA.
  • It is a depth effect. Shallow prefixes are mostly difficulty, deep prefixes mostly are not. Averaging over depth hid this.
  • "Just counting tokens" is a scale artefact. On a log scale we beat prefix length at every cut on all three datasets.

Questions for you

  • Which is the paper? "Question-level but not text-recoverable", or the depth effect? They point at different experiments next.
  • Is MMLU-Pro's entropy margin enough? It is significant but its interval edge is 0.001 from zero. More rows, or accept it?
  • Commitment is our least settled target. Accuracy and AUC disagree on GlobalOpinionQA, and the AUC sign flips on an arbitrary grid choice (+0.009 or -0.003 against TF-IDF). It was also never partialled. Fix the convention, or drop it?
  • Third dataset, or go deeper on these two?
  • The strongest baseline is still another LLM's activations, not text. Should that be the headline comparison?

Accuracy and AUC disagree in sign on GlobalOpinionQA (accuracy 0.690 ours against 0.693 TF-IDF, AUC 0.683 against 0.674); the AUC depends on which logistic grid was used and nothing in the files records it (ours 0.683 shallow, 0.671 deep, which flips our advantage from +0.009 significant to -0.003 not significant); the ICL AUC check that overturned "commitment ties" on GPQA has not been run on either new dataset; and at GPQA's 90 percent cut the accuracy is just the base rate. That is the comparison a referee will reach for, and we have never run it on the new datasets.
