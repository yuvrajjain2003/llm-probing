# Round 6, part 1: the frontier model's tie is question difficulty. And why examples hurt.

_2026-10-29. Audience: the team. Everything here cost zero GPU hours and zero dollars: it is all
re-analysis of predictions already on disk. Code: `src/analysis/icl_decompose.py`,
`src/analysis/icl_anchoring.py`, `src/analysis/icl_bands.py`. Outputs:
`runs/goal2/gpqa25/sweep/_iclsplit/` and `runs/goal2/gpqa25/sweep/_iclwhy/`._

## The short version

1. **Concern 2 is settled, and the uncomfortable reading is the right one.** When question difficulty
   is removed, our probe's answer-entropy R2 falls from 0.309 to 0.092 and GPT-6 Luna's falls from
   0.294 to 0.037. Neither is significantly above zero any more. Remaining length barely moves: ours
   0.563 to 0.528, Luna 0.455 to 0.397, and our advantage stays significant. Shown two independent
   ways (partialling difficulty out, and scoring inside difficulty bands).
2. **Concern 1 has a mechanism.** In-context examples teach the model the output *scale* but degrade
   its *ranking*. Within-cut correlation falls versus zero-shot in **28 of 28** comparisons across two
   model families, mean drop 0.131. Because the published comparison calibrates the predictions, the
   scale benefit is given away for free and only the ranking damage remains. That is why k=0 wins.
3. **Action 2 needs no money.** A query prompt does not depend on the difficulty band, so restricting
   the stored predictions to one band is the same measurement as rerunning Luna on it. Done, $0 spent.
4. **New finding that changes a published conclusion.** On `committed_by_here` the in-context models
   tie us on accuracy (0.718 to 0.742 against our 0.727, majority baseline 0.634) but lose clearly on
   **AUC**: ours 0.773 against 0.651 to 0.677 for every ICL configuration, a gap of 0.10 to 0.12 that
   is significant in all nine. The round-5 statement that commitment "ties" was an artefact of using
   accuracy alone.

Validation: the re-analysis reproduces every published number in `sweep/_icl/ci_scores.csv` to
1.1e-15 over 28 cells, and part 1's structural counts come straight from the stored predictions.

## What could not be done, and why

The ask was to apply round 4's between-question / within-question decomposition to Luna's stored
predictions. **That decomposition is degenerate on the ICL row set.** The in-context runs use one CoT
per question per cut, so within-question sums of squares are exactly zero and every cut's R2 is 100%
between-question by construction, for Luna and for our probe alike
(`_iclsplit/p1_row_structure.csv`):

| row set | rows per cut | questions | within-question share of total SS |
|---|---|---|---|
| ICL rows (1 CoT/question) | 196 | 196 | 0.000 at every cut |
| full `ref_fixed` rows | ~4,913 | 198 | 0.183, 0.291, 0.394, 0.473, 0.572 at 10 to 90% |

So "Luna's R2 is purely between-question" is true but empty. The more useful consequence is this:
**the headline round-5 comparison, Luna 0.294 against our 0.309 on entropy, was never a test of
within-question skill at all.** It only ever compared between-question skill.

## Concern 2: remove question difficulty and the entropy tie disappears

Difficulty is the 0% cut's `p_gold`, the fraction of the 25 base CoTs that were correct, entered as a
quadratic so its non-monotonic shape is allowed for. Both the target and each prediction are
residualised against it, within each cut, then scored and averaged over the five cuts. Question
bootstrap, 2,000 draws, the quadratic refitted inside every draw.

| | entropy R2 | entropy, difficulty removed | rem_len_mean R2 | rem_len_mean, difficulty removed |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Our probe (L21 ridge) | 0.309 | **0.092 [-0.010, 0.180]** | 0.563 | **0.528 [0.469, 0.587]** |
| GPT-6 Luna, k=0 | 0.294 | **0.037 [-0.072, 0.140]** | 0.455 | 0.397 [0.313, 0.475] |
| Gemma-4-31B, k=0 | 0.262 | 0.045 | 0.283 | 0.212 |
| Prefix length | 0.247 | 0.121 [0.008, 0.221] | 0.552 | 0.509 [0.441, 0.571] |

Read the entropy column pair first. **Every method loses roughly three quarters of its entropy R2**,
and afterwards neither the best probe nor Luna is significantly above zero. The only predictor
still significantly above zero on entropy is prefix length, which is a within-chain quantity rather
than a question-level one. Now read the length column pair. **Nothing much happens.** Our probe keeps
0.528 of 0.563, and it still beats Luna by 0.131 [0.070, 0.195] after difficulty is gone.

The same pattern on the other two targets: `p_mode` 0.261 to 0.060 for us and 0.233 to 0.019 for Luna;
`rem_len_std` 0.328 to 0.325 for us and 0.122 to 0.107 for Luna. So the split is not "entropy versus
length" by accident, it is "answer-distribution shape at the question level" versus "where this
particular chain is".

**Caveat, and it matters.** `p_gold` is computed from rollouts, so it is label-derived and not a
feature available at prediction time. Residualising against it is the right tool for sizing a
confound but it over-removes: any genuine signal correlated with difficulty goes with it. So the
numbers above are a **lower bound** on what survives, not a measurement of usable skill. The
legitimate version is a difficulty estimate built only from the question text, which is still unrun.

### How the difficulty bands are defined

Each of the 198 GPQA questions was attempted 25 times independently by the model, at the very start of
the chain before any reasoning was written. The question's difficulty is simply **how many of those 25
attempts got the right answer**. That number is already in the 0% cut's labels as `p_gold`, so no new
compute was needed. The 198 questions are then sorted by it and cut into five equal groups.

| band | attempts correct, out of 25 | questions | mean answer entropy | mean remaining length |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| very hard | 0 to 3 | 40 | 0.81 | 2,896 |
| hard | 3 to 7 | 39 | 1.09 | 3,639 |
| medium | 8 to 15 | 40 | 1.09 | 3,462 |
| easy | 15 to 23 | 39 | 0.63 | 2,837 |
| very easy | 23 to 25 | 40 | 0.10 | 1,603 |

Across the whole set the mean is 12.3 of 25 correct, and both ends are populated: 17 questions are
never right and 28 are always right.

**The one thing to say out loud when presenting this: difficulty and uncertainty are NOT the same
thing.** Entropy rises from very easy to medium and then falls again, because on the hardest questions
the model is confidently wrong. Anything that assumes "harder means more uncertain" is wrong at the top
end.

Two caveats on the construction. Equal group sizes were chosen so that a score difference between bands
is not confounded with sample size, and the cost is that questions with the *same* number of correct
attempts can land either side of a boundary: 8 of the 198 do, at the 3, 15 and 23 marks, which is why
the ranges above overlap. Re-cutting so that tied questions always share a band changes the entropy
figures by at most 0.08 and changes no conclusion (very hard +0.157, hard +0.046, medium -0.067, easy
+0.058 for the probe, against +0.131 / +0.057 / -0.143 / +0.096 as published). Second, `p_gold` is
computed from rollouts, so it is a *label-derived* measure of difficulty and not a feature available at
prediction time. That is fine for splitting the data, which is all it is used for here, but it is why it
must never be quoted as a baseline.

### The band version, at zero cost

Action 2 was budgeted at a few dollars to rerun Luna inside one difficulty band. It needs no API
calls. The query prompt in `icl_openai.py` does not depend on the band, and the in-context examples
are drawn by `example_order` from all training-fold questions regardless of band, so restricting the
stored predictions to a band is exactly the same measurement. That also disposes of the calibration
worry in `NEXT_STEPS.md`: `icl_cal` was fitted per fold on the full other-fold rows, so nothing is
refitted on a 3-question cell.

Using `band_quintile` (equal sizes, about 39 questions each), mean over cuts:

**These are offset corrected.** The stored predictions come from a fit on all 198 questions and are
never recentred per band, so an uncorrected band score is charged for an offset the full-set fit never
had to carry. Matching the prediction's mean to the truth's inside each band costs one degree of
freedom and leaves the slope alone, so under-confidence is still penalised.

| entropy R2 | all questions | v_hard | hard | medium | easy | v_easy |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Best probe | +0.311 | +0.148 | +0.174 | +0.108 | +0.110 | -1.09 |
| Luna k=0 | +0.294 | +0.062 | +0.143 | -0.071 | +0.142 | -0.49 |
| Prefix length | +0.248 | +0.180 | +0.172 | -0.076 | +0.210 | -0.35 |

| rem_len_mean R2 | all questions | v_hard | hard | medium | easy | v_easy |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Best probe | +0.563 | +0.591 | +0.564 | +0.519 | +0.512 | +0.491 |
| Luna k=0 | +0.455 | +0.537 | +0.429 | +0.350 | +0.283 | +0.501 |
| Prefix length | +0.552 | +0.585 | +0.577 | +0.436 | +0.427 | +0.557 |

Entropy falls from about 0.30 to between 0.11 and 0.17 inside every band; remaining length does not
move. CI half-widths are 0.14 to 0.26 for entropy inside a band, so no single band is decisive on its
own and it is the agreement across all five that carries it.

The very-easy band stays negative even after the offset correction, and that part is **real, not an
artefact**: there the truth is nearly constant (sd 0.265 against 0.642 overall) while the predictions
go on varying as much as ever, so they add error rather than removing it. The most forgiving view
available drops the scale as well as the offset, and tells the same story:

| within-cut correlation | all questions | v_hard | hard | medium | easy | v_easy |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| entropy, our probe | 0.559 | 0.392 | 0.422 | 0.382 | 0.398 | 0.234 |
| entropy, Luna k=0 | 0.542 | 0.321 | 0.416 | 0.193 | 0.430 | 0.356 |
| rem_len_mean, our probe | 0.753 | 0.779 | 0.757 | 0.722 | 0.730 | 0.719 |
| rem_len_mean, Luna k=0 | 0.666 | 0.728 | 0.649 | 0.611 | 0.521 | 0.723 |

Entropy correlation drops by about a third inside a band for both of us. Length correlation is
**flat** for the best probe across all five bands, and it is ahead of Luna in every band.

**One more check, since R2 near zero is ambiguous.** It can mean "predicts a constant" or "predicts
well but far too timidly", and here it is the second. Inside a band the predictions still vary about
half as much as the truth (sd ratio 0.47 to 0.55 on entropy; a constant predictor would be 0) and
still correlate with it. The failure is in the mean: true entropy by band, very hard to very easy, is
0.807 / 1.086 / 1.086 / 0.627 / **0.100**, while the probe says 0.869 / 0.905 / 0.861 / 0.667 /
**0.404** and Luna says 0.947 / 0.941 / 0.910 / 0.656 / **0.365**. Both compress a 0.99-bit range into
about 0.50 and are four times too high on the easiest questions. Remaining length behaves the opposite
way on both counts. See `fig12_predicting_the_mean.png` and `_iclsplit/p7_band_spread.csv`.

So the prediction table written down in `NEXT_STEPS.md` before any of this was run is confirmed on
both of its rows: stratifying kills the entropy tie and leaves our length advantage intact.

## Why pooled R2 is positive while every band is near zero

This is the question the figures provoke, and the arithmetic answers it exactly. Split the spread in
the truth into the part that sits between the five bands and the part that sits within them, and score
the probe on each part separately. Pooled over the five cuts, 196 questions:

| | answer entropy | remaining length |
|---|---|---|
| share of the spread sitting BETWEEN bands | 33.0% | 16.5% |
| share sitting WITHIN bands | 67.0% | 83.5% |
| probe R2 on the between-band part | +0.702 | +0.826 |
| probe R2 on the within-band part | +0.116 | +0.568 |
| **pooled R2** | **+0.310** | **+0.610** |

The pooled number is just the weighted sum: 0.330 x 0.702 + 0.670 x 0.116 = 0.310 for entropy, and
0.165 x 0.826 + 0.835 x 0.568 = 0.610 for length. Both reproduce the published figures.

So nothing is inconsistent. On entropy the probe is very good at the third of the spread that is
difficulty (0.702) and close to useless on the other two thirds (0.116), and the pooled score is
flattered because the easy third is weighted heavily by how much variance it carries. On remaining
length the between-band part is only a sixth of the spread, and the probe is still at 0.568 on the
other five sixths, which is why nothing collapses when you stratify.

One reading note. The per-band R2 values in the figure are lower than the 0.116 here, and often
negative, because each band is scored against its own small variance and the predictions are never
recentred per band. The 0.116 pools the five bands with a common recentring, so it is the fairer
summary of within-band skill. Both say the same thing: the entropy signal is mostly difficulty.

## Concern 1: examples fix the scale and break the ranking

Measured on the **raw, uncalibrated** predictions, because calibration rescales per fold and would
hide exactly the effect at issue (`_iclwhy/anchoring.csv`, `_iclwhy/vs_zero_shot.csv`).

Across the 28 comparisons where a k=0 control exists for the same model and prompt version (Luna
`ckpt`, Luna `blind`, Gemma `ckpt`, four targets each):

- **Within-cut correlation falls versus k=0 in 28 of 28**, mean drop 0.131, worst 0.335. By target:
  entropy -0.075, p_mode -0.056, rem_len_mean -0.244, rem_len_std -0.147.
- **The prediction spread moves closer to the truth's spread in 19 of 28**, and raw R2 improves in 15
  of 28, mean +0.40. The clearest cases are where zero-shot had no idea of the scale at all: Gemma's
  rem_len_mean spread ratio goes from 0.057 to 0.99, and Luna `blind` from 0.197 to 0.78.

That is the mechanism. Examples do teach the model something real, the output scale, and they damage
something else, the ordering of rows within a cut. Our published comparison calibrates, which hands
the model the scale for free, so the benefit is invisible and only the damage counts. Hence k=0 wins,
in both families.

On the two named hypotheses:

- **Anchoring, in the classic shrink-toward-the-examples sense, is not what happens.** Spread widens
  rather than narrowing, and the mean distance from a prediction to the example set's mean stays at
  0.6 to 1.0 target sd. The model is not averaging the examples.
- **Verbatim copying of example values is real but small, and only on entropy.** Against a chance
  baseline (the same model's k=0 predictions matched to the example values that fold would have been
  shown), the excess exact-match rate is +0.08 to +0.29 for entropy at every k in both families, and
  about zero for both length targets. Without the chance baseline this looks far bigger than it is:
  Gemma's raw p_mode match rate reaches 0.94 at k=93, but chance is 0.955, because p_mode is shown to
  two decimals on a short range. The length targets are integers in the thousands, where copying is
  detectable and essentially absent, yet the ranking still degrades badly there (Gemma rem_len_mean
  correlation 0.547 at k=0 against 0.234 at k=10). **So copying contributes and does not explain it.**
- **The noisy-example-label hypothesis cannot be tested with these runs.** `example_order` depends
  only on (fold, k), so every test row in a fold saw an identical example set. That leaves five
  example sets per configuration, and the fold-level correlations between example label noise and
  error are inconsistent in sign (`_iclwhy/h2_example_noise_by_fold.csv`). Testing it needs a run
  that varies example noise by design.

**Consequence for ask 4 (GEPA).** Example selection optimises toward k=0 and is not worth running.
Instruction optimisation is untouched by this result and remains open.

## Commitment: report AUC, not accuracy

| | accuracy | AUC | AUC minus ours |
|---|---|---|---|
| Our probe (L21 logistic) | 0.727 | **0.773** | |
| Prefix length | 0.737 | 0.761 | -0.012 [-0.052, 0.031] |
| Luna k=0 | 0.718 | 0.666 | **-0.107 [-0.151, -0.063]** |
| Luna k=50 | 0.720 | 0.659 | **-0.114 [-0.165, -0.066]** |
| Gemma-4-31B k=0 | 0.742 | 0.663 | **-0.110 [-0.162, -0.062]** |

Majority-class baseline 0.634. All nine in-context configurations are significantly below our probe on
AUC while tying or beating it on accuracy. The accuracy column is close to the base rate for everyone,
so it has very little resolving power on this target. This is the second time the AUC convention has
changed a conclusion on this target, after the round-5 finding that commitment accuracy at the 90% cut
is just the base rate.

## The one thing none of this settles

**Our probe's numbers are uncalibrated and the in-context numbers are calibrated** (`actual = a + b x
predicted`, fitted per fold on the other folds' rows). That is defensible, because at k=0 the model
was never told the scale and penalising it for information we withheld would be unfair, but it must be
stated in any write-up. Raw, Luna is far below zero on most targets. The concern-1 analysis above is
in a sense the price of that choice made visible: calibration is exactly the service the in-context
examples were trying to provide.

## What this means for the paper

Round 4's characterisation survives a completely independent test and should now be stated plainly:

> Activations beat text at judging **which questions** will produce uncertain rollouts. They do not
> beat text at judging **which chain of a given question** will.

With one sharpening. The entropy and `p_mode` half of that sentence is now better described as
**difficulty reading**, and a frontier model with no activations and no examples does it just as well.
The part that is ours, and that survives both difficulty removal and band stratification, is
**remaining length**: 0.528 of 0.563 after difficulty is partialled out, flat across all five
difficulty bands, and significantly ahead of every in-context model.
